## Proposal being examined

The user's proposal is that a theory needs a name or ID so that evidence can count against it as an identifiable object, beyond the particular commitments invoked in one prediction. The same reference supports true statements of what follows "according to" that theory, while the theory remains empirically falsifiable.

## Proposed reconstruction

A stable reference lets us retain one definite theory instance across its entries, predictive traces, revisions, and evidence. The reference is more than a title when it identifies which commitments count as the same target. For an append-only AD, a historically precise target may require the dictionary ID together with its stage/version and the entry choices used in the trace. A name attached to a changing unspecified collection does not itself supply this stability.

Strict necessity attaches to identifiable content, not to having a proper name. An explicitly displayed anonymous set of statements can be contradicted and described truthfully. A name, number, demonstrative, or complete specification can provide the requisite reference. If "name" means any such stable handle, the user's claim is considerably stronger than if it means a literal title.

There are two distinct assertions:

1. Given the fixed theory T, local assumptions A, and a valid derivation, prediction p follows.
2. The actual observed situation satisfies the relevant commitments and produces p.

A successful proof or faithful attribution can establish (1) while experiment undermines (2). Prefixing "according to T" does not by itself make an arbitrary assertion true or logically tautological; the attribution or derivation must be checked. The relevant mathematical feature is consequence conditional on specified premises and rules. The formal derivation can remain correct precisely when observation counts against the applicability or truth of its empirical premises.

For the elementary deterministic case, (T AND A) implies p, together with an accepted observation of NOT p, gives NOT (T AND A). It does not identify which premise failed. Naming T records the candidate target but does not solve the allocation of failure between T and A. If the falsified conjunction is itself the named complete model/theory instance, that package is the immediate target. Refuting a package never means every one of its component statements has independently been refuted.

## Interaction with the prediction-set notes

The current synthesis's sections 9-11 turn trace commitments into diagnostic branches: wrong input, computation, frame, incomplete force account, missing source, approximation failure, undefined referent, or theory failure. This is a framework for investigating a discrepancy. The stable theory reference lets the investigation ask which changes preserve T and which produce a revised theory T'.

A distinction is needed between:

- the observations compatible with a fixed T under its admissible, evidenced auxiliary assumptions; and
- the complete diagnostic ledger, which can also include the conclusion that T fails.

The source uses "prediction set" for the broader diagnostic ledger. That usage can be retained, but theory failure must not be counted as a successful empirical prediction of T. Including "T is false" as a universal escape branch and calling the disjunction T's prediction would remove the exclusions needed for falsifiability.

A schematic deterministic outcome set is P_T(C) = union over admissible auxiliaries A consistent with accepted conditions/evidence C of the outcomes permitted by (T,A). This is a proposed reconstruction, not notation supplied by the documents. A possible empirical falsifier requires outcomes that the fixed account rules out; if unrestricted auxiliary repair makes P_T(C) include everything, a stable ID does not restore falsifiability. Statistical testing additionally needs probabilities or another warranted evidential rule; permitted-versus-excluded membership alone is insufficient.

Evidence can count against a theory before all theory-preserving branches are eliminated. The stronger question of when to reject it should not be conflated with whether its evidential standing has worsened. Nor should the source's actual-production-path discipline be taken here as a general Bayesian theorem excluding all updates to theories not causally used to generate a forecast; that broader assertion was not validated in this reading.

## Emerging claim and remaining work

Proposed claim: A named Authority Dictionary makes a theory's commitments identifiable, allowing its consequences to be established conditionally and its empirical performance to be assessed against the same specified target. Prediction-set diagnosis makes the theory-preserving alternatives explicit; falsifiability additionally requires that those alternatives leave observable ways for the target to fail.

The remaining substantive task is to state what counts as preserving an AD/theory instance across model repair, what changes count as a revision, and what evidence constrains the available repair branches. Naming makes these questions expressible and auditable; naming alone does not answer them.

## Follow-up: past evidence, predictive skill, and credit to the theory

Added 2026-09-08 after the next user message. Status remains exploratory. The user proposed that conclusions based on past evidence expire; successful prediction first establishes the predictor's success; evidence should improve belief in the theory to the extent that the theory determined the tested hypothesis. They asked whether the link is a Markov chain or gradient descent.

The key additional local source is `07_design_2_1/notes/conversation_captures/CONVERSATION_ENTRY_CONSIDERATIONS_2026-05-14.md`, Global Orientation, which describes a theory as writings/strings that increase a trained person's probability of predicting the future correctly. `COMBINING_REACTIONS_FOR_PREDICTION.md` develops the same account as controlled presentation of stimuli and trained responses. These are local conceptual/historical captures, not current entry or force-naming authority. The managed current prediction-set synthesis, sections 1-2, was re-read from the same pinned snapshot held in this task. Its production-path claim is directly relevant but needs the qualification below.

Proposed distinctions:

1. A past successful test remains part of the historical evidence. Its licence to predict a new situation is conditional on the stability or transportability of the relevant conditions, instruments, modelling assumptions, and procedure. Passage of time alone does not imply a universal evidential expiry rule. The local E10 d4 packet's prohibition on automatically reusing expired bindings is an operational analogy, not proof that all theory evidence expires. One correct forecast establishes success on that occasion, not reliable predictive ability in general.
2. Bayesian updating is a formal rule for revising beliefs from evidence. A probabilistic dependency graph can organise the variables and support inference; a Markov chain is only a special structure with additional independence assumptions. Gradient descent is an optimisation algorithm, not the evidential justification for believing a theory. These tools are not interchangeable names for the same link.
3. The named AD fixes the candidate account; a trace records which entries, interpreter responses, data, and auxiliary assumptions produced the forecast. This is attribution/provenance evidence. Quantifying empirical support additionally needs the probability of the observations under the account and relevant alternatives, including uncertainty over auxiliaries. The trace alone does not provide a likelihood or a percentage of confirmation.
4. Two empirical targets need distinguishing: whether the theory/model's predictive content fits the world, and whether access to this AD improves a specified class of interpreters' predictive performance. The latter is a causal contribution question. Comparative performance under appropriately controlled access to the AD can investigate it; faithful derivation alone does not establish improvement over the predictor's existing competence. No experiment has been designed or run here.
5. Causal use and evidential discrimination differ. A theory can determine a forecast while a rival predicts exactly the same result; success then gives no likelihood-ratio advantage between those two accounts. Conversely, observations can favour a theory's specified predictions even if those observations were first collected for a different reason or a person guessed the forecast independently. Data selection, prior information, and post-outcome tailoring still need accounting. Therefore the current synthesis's absolute wording "No production path, no theory support" is too strong as a general Bayesian principle. Its defensible role is a discipline against misattributing a predictor's success to a theory that was not used, not a universal condition for empirical confirmation of predictive content.
6. The synthesis's chain T -> model -> computation -> hypothesis -> observation is useful as a workflow sketch but is not automatically a justified Bayesian factorisation or causal graph. A forecast normally does not cause the physical outcome it forecasts. A probability model must distinguish theory text/use from the hypothesis that its account describes the world, represent observational and procedural uncertainties, and justify its conditional-independence assumptions. Merely drawing the arrows does not validate backward transmission of support.

Illustrative Bayesian comparison, with old evidence and experimental context held fixed: posterior odds of T versus U equal prior odds times P(new observation | T, context) / P(new observation | U, context). If those likelihoods are .9 and .1, the odds multiply by 9; if both are .9, the odds do not change. This is not a formula assigning credit according to the number of invoked entries. Probabilistic theories may substantially constrain an outcome distribution without determining its realised value.

External primary reference checked: [Kass and Raftery (1995), Bayes Factors](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/courses/stat527/s13/readings/KassRaftery_jasa1995.pdf), for model comparison through data probabilities, prior odds, and posterior odds. The proposed VD attribution distinction is our analysis, not a claim made by that paper.
