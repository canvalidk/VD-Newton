# Why the Authority Dictionary's name matters

## User's correction

The user stressed that statements are supposed to be true "according to the *name of the dictionary*" and asked why the name mattered in the existing documents.

## Reconstruction

The name identifies the authority under which the dictionary's statements are made. Reading an entry includes an implicit "according to [dictionary name]". A faithful statement of a dictionary's definition is assessable by reference to that dictionary. Endorsing that attribution is a different commitment from endorsing the definition as ordinary usage or endorsing a represented theory as empirically accurate.

This applies even to foundational vocabulary such as "definition", "meaning", and "physical law". The early how-to explicitly installs those as definitions IN SDVD. They can be discussed as determinate commitments of a named authority without first claiming universally correct meanings for those words.

The name also lets different authorities be proposed, compared, and disagreed with. It makes it possible to distinguish a disagreement about what D says from a disagreement with D, and a disagreement about vocabulary from an empirical disagreement. The chosen spelling may be arbitrary; identifying the authority is necessary to know whose commitments are being stated.

The exact broad formula "every statement is true according to the dictionary's name" is the user's current clarification. The sources directly establish authority indexing of definitional claims, a name that establishes scope of authority, and the separation of represented commitments from empirical truth. This reading does not claim a theorem that arbitrary inference, inconsistent entries, or unreliable external responses become valid just by attaching a name.

## Direct evidence

- The early how-to, section 2.1, calls the dictionary a named authority and says a definitional statement is ill-posed without its explicit or implicit name. Section 2.2 explains contestable, replicable authorities. Sections 3.1-3.3 explicitly give definitions IN SDVD; section 7.4 separates definitional from empirical disagreement.
- VD_Justifications3, section 3.1 (page 4), defines D = (Name, L) and says the Name "establishes the scope of authority". This version already identifies a word's definition with the complete history of its transactions rather than one output string.
- The protocol and amendments retain the named-object structure. They corroborate it; they are historical development material, not a reason to reinstate every old implementation rule.
- The authority-documents note, sections 2-3, separates the naming arrangement represented by a document from whether a community actually realises that arrangement. It supports the distinction between determinate represented content and external correctness. It is a current working derivation with explicitly provisional parts, not a completed derivation of VD.
- The current language-problem draft, sections 2 and 5, discusses acknowledging the other party's authority over their usage. This is supporting philosophical context, not an exact formal definition of AD.

## Correction to the visual explainer

The PDF created earlier in this task accurately illustrated the list/entry/headword/definiens/definition relationships but omitted the dictionary's own name and its authority function. Calling the object only an ordered list leaves out the condition the user now foregrounds. A revised introductory account should describe a NAMED ordered list of entries and explicitly show how its name supplies "according to [name]" for the entries. The example requires its own dictionary name, distinct from the generic type label "Authority Dictionary". No PDF revision was made during this research request.

The 2026-09-04 local foundational reading record already says that a named dictionary makes the authority behind a definitional claim explicit. That observation had not been carried through into the generated explainer. The issue is an omitted conceptual consequence, not absence of any earlier mention.
