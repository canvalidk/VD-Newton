Scope of this paper.We treat the SDVD as an append-only construction:
entries are added in a fixed order and no in-place edits are permitted. We
therefore do not propose a governance theory of error-correction (renaming,
deprecation, merge/split policies, etc.) in this work. If an author wishes to
revise an entry, the intended procedure here is to restart the build from a clean
log. A separate follow-up will study admissible update policies and their effect
on recognisable law-formation.
Important clarifierWe need to make sure readers understand that entries to
the VD can be made even though the entries are not physical laws. An example
might be ”mass1 = the mass associated with particle 1.” or ”spring extension
= the amount a spring is displaced by from its equilibrium position. ”

We treat all recognition predicates (e.g.Triplet) as purely syntactic: they
operate on strings.
Multi-valued lookup (polysemy) and whyD: Strings→Stringsis too
strong.In the early presentation it is tempting to model a dictionary as a
functionD: Strings→Strings, sending each wordwto a unique definition-
stringD(w). However, this single-valued model is incompatible with the in-
tended SDVD workflow: (i) the construction isappend-only(entries are added
in a linear order), and (ii) a word may legitimately acquire multiple simulta-
neously active definition-strings (e.g. distinct senses, regimes, approximations,
or competing proposals). If we insist onDbeing a function, then whenever a
word is defined twice we must quietly introduce an additional policy (“latest
definition wins”, “first definition wins”, “deprecated definitions are inactive”,
etc.). Such a policy is precisely the kind of dictionary-modification rule we aim
toavoidin this paper.
Entry-log as primitive object.We therefore take the primitive object to
be the entry-log rather than a single-valued map. An entry is a pair
En := (wn, δn)∈Strings×Strings,
and a stage-ndictionary state is the finite set (or ordered list)
D(n) :={(w k, δk) : 0≤k≤n}.
Lookup is induced from the log as amulti-map(equivalently, a relation)
D(n)[w] :={δ∈Strings : (w, δ)∈ D (n)} ⊆Strings,
or, when order matters, as the list ofδ k with headwordw k ≡win increasing
log indexk.
Why this change is necessary (and not an extra rule).AllowingD (n)[w]
to contain multiple definition-strings does not add a new governance rule; it
removes an implicit one. The append-only log already determineswhat com-
mitments have been made(the multiset of entries), and multi-valued lookup

Consequences for “previously defined” and law-recognition.With the
log as primitive, “previously defined” is a statement about headwords in the log:
n(w) :⇐ ⇒ ∃k < nsuch thatw k ≡w.
Law-recognition likewise remains entry-indexed: a triplet is a property of three
entries (Ei, Ej, Ek), not of a globally unique valueD(w). This keeps the formal
system faithful to the guiding principle that SDVD commitments are syntactic
(strings in an ordered log), while deferring semantic questions of consistency or
preferred usage to later work.
Unique-November constraint (one November word per law).In ad-
dition to the entry-log and triplet recognition rules, we impose a simple unique-
ness constraint onlaw anchors. Each recognised physical law has a designated
Novemberheadword (the previously-logged term that is locked to two newly
introduced terms). We require that no headword may serve as the November
word for more than one law.
Formally, letLdenote the set of recognised law-events (triplets), and let
Nov :L →Strings return the November headword of a law-event. Theunique-
Novemberconstraint is

Hypergraph interpretation.Represent each law-eventL∈ Las a hyper-
edge incident to the participating headwords, and mark one incident vertex as
the November vertex. The unique-November constraint is equivalent to stating
that the November-marked incidence relation forms a matching: each word-
vertex has November-degree at most one. This endows the law-subgraph with
a clean provenance structure (a unique “anchor” per law) while leaving the full
entry-log free to contain additional, non-law definitions of the same headwords.

when in code.

2 Entrenched VD Insights from the Newtonian
Case Study
The following are VD-specificpractice rulesandstructural insightsdistilled from
our attempt to encode Newtonian mechanics. These are not claims about what
is true in nature; they are commitments about how we represent and audit a
theory in the VD.
1.Separate bookkeeping from attribution.
•Bookkeeping:definitions such asinteracting forces setandnet force
specify how contributions are aggregated once they are included.
•Attribution:deciding which contributions are included is a meaning-
level recognition step unless a specific mechanism is explicitly de-
clared.
•Rule:never allow an aggregate term (e.g.net force) to silently
imply a causal source. Causality attribution must be a separate layer.
2.Do not infer interaction from kinematics alone.
•Trajectories that look like collisions do not uniquely determine whether
particles interacted; particles can approach and later separate with-
out interaction (e.g. passing through in a toy model).
•Conservation of total momentum for a subsystem does not imply
internal interaction; it only implies vanishing net external impulse
on that subsystem.
•Rule:treat “is interacting” as a modeling decision unless the inter-
action structure is explicitly represented.
3.Localize irreducible complexity into recognition.
•The hardest part of mechanics is not the algebra but the recognition
step: system boundary choice, contact detection, regime selection,
and deciding what is “in” the interaction set.
•Rule:the only allowed vagueness is in recognition predicates; once
recognition inputs are declared, all downstream steps must be me-
chanically executable and auditable.
4.Use explicit intermediate bridge words to avoid smuggling intu-
ition.
•Newton I becomes structurally clearer when one introduces an ex-
plicit bridge term such asuniform motion on interval(I)oruniform motion at time(t0)
rather than relying on tacit acceleration intuition.
•Rule:when a law depends on an intuition that readers typically
import silently, introduce and lock an explicit bridge word.

5.Arguments must appear in the headword for function-like terms.
•Rule:if a term behaves like a function or predicate of parame-
ters (time, particle labels, intervals), include those parameters in
the headword (schema style), e.g.uniform motion at time(t0)not
uniform motion at time.
•Motivation: prevents hidden/free variables and makes the dictionary
log auditable.
6.Triplets are for commitments; raw-meaning entries are for exe-
cution semantics.
•Triplets lock a stable mutual relationship between terms (e.g.raw class,
class specific features,instance of).
•Raw-meaning entries specify human-executable procedures (e.g. book-
keeping steps, recognition inputs, update rules) without attempting
to define interpretability primitives.
•Rule:use triplets when you want immovability; use raw-meaning
when you want operational instructions.
7.Track “degree of law” as a structural dependency metric.
•Definition (informal):a law is higher-degree if its November term
was previously introduced as a winter term of an earlier law; degree
measures layering.
•Rule:record degree as part of the audit trail to make compositional
structure visible.
8.Maintain strict control of November words (bridge discipline).
•Rule:a November word is a deliberate anchor; do not promote a
term to November unless you intend to anchor a new commitment.
•Keep a ledger of November usage to prevent accidental reuse and
semantic drift.
9.Interpretability is assumed; do not over-define.
•We do not define basic interpretive operations (e.g. arithmetic, sum-
mation, human recognition) inside the VD; we treat them as meaning-
level competence, as withF=mawhere multiplication is not de-
fined.
•Rule:avoid adding dictionary entries whose only purpose is to justify
interpretability; prefer explicit bridge terms only when they remove
ambiguity about dependencies.
10.Distinguish existence from activation (presence vs activity).

•A modeled entity can exist even when its instantaneous contribution
is zero (e.g. a spring exists even if not stretched).
•Rule:represent “object exists” separately from “object is active /
contributes now,” so that conceptual presence is not conflated with
force magnitude.
11.Avoid inferring causal source from net quantities.
•net forceis an aggregate; equal-and-oppositenet forces do not
identify whether the source was mutual interaction, mediation by a
third object, or boundary exchange.
•Rule:causal/source claims require explicit contributory structure
(e.g. force-terms or later acting-object instances), not merely net
equalities.
12.System boundaries must be explicit when action–reaction is dis-
cussed.
•Momentum conservation and Newton III style reasoning depend on
whether reaction partners are included in the system.
•Rule:whenever a reaction term is not represented within the modeled
system, the omission must be stated as an explicit boundary/externalization
choice.
