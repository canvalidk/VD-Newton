SDVD / Valid Dictionary (VD): First Draft Notes
1 Motivation and scope
These notes are a first draft of a proposal for formalising how scientific language is built and
used. The core claim is not that we have discovered the “true meanings” of words, but that we
can write down a disciplinedstructurein which statements about words, definitions, and laws
are made explicit and therefore debateable, comparable, and repairable.
The proposed framework is theValid Dictionary(VD), implemented as a named dictionary
authority. A concrete instance used throughout is theSuper Duper Valid Dictionary(SDVD), a
deliberately unobtrusive name intended to avoid premature sociological escalation (“this is the
one true dictionary of science”) while still enabling precise reference.
2 VD as a named data structure
2.1 Dictionary object and the necessity of a name
At the most primitive level, a dictionary is a data structure:
D: Strings→Strings,
mapping an input string (a “word”) to an output string (its dictionary entry).
A subtle but foundational point is that, in practice, one cannot speak about “the definition
of a word” without implicitly relying on anauthority—some specific dictionary or convention.
The VD proposal makes that authority explicit by treating the dictionary as anamed object.
Definition 1(Named dictionary authority).Anamed dictionaryis a pair( Name(D), D)where
Name(D)∈StringsandD: Strings→Strings.
Remark 1(Authority indexing).Statements of the form “the definition of w is δ” should be
read as
“In dictionaryD,D(w) =δ.”
Without the dictionary name (explicit or implicit), the statement is ill-posed.
2.2 Democratising authority
Once authority is explicit, it is alsocontestable and replicable. One may propose SDVD, an
alternative VD2, or any other named dictionary, and compare them as objects. This turns many
philosophical disputes into explicit disagreements about dictionary entries and their induced
behaviour, rather than disputes conducted under a hidden background authority.
Entries, ordinality, and the zeroth entry.Having fixed anameddictionary D : Σ∗ → Σ∗
(and hence an explicit locus of authority for what is “meant” by each word), we must also specify
what it means toaddcontent to D. We therefore treat each addition as anentry-event: at
some stage n we submit an entry En to the dictionary, producing an updated dictionary D(n)
which extends D(n−1). This induces an ordinal notion of priority: one entry isearlierthan

another if it was submitted at a prior stage. Numbering the entries by conventional integers
is not logically necessary (the global structure is a hypergraph of cross-references), but some
well-founded bookkeeping of “earlier vs later” is practically indispensable, because our later rules

this in place, we designate a canonical starting point: theraw definitionentry is taken to be
thezeroth entry E0, which fixes the primitive notion of definition used throughout. Concretely,
E0 asserts that adefinitionis the output string associated to a word by the dictionary, and
that two raw definitions are equal iff the corresponding strings are character-identical. From
this point onward, all subsequent entries are logged relative to this baseline, so that claims like
“word w was previously defined” become precise statements about whether w occurred in the
accumulated log{E 0, E1, . . . , En−1}at the time an entryE n was added.
3 The first three SDVD entries
We now install three core entries in SDVD. They act as type-setting primitives: they specify
what sort of objects “definitions”, “meanings”, and “physical laws” are taken to bein this
framework.
3.1 Definition:definitionis the dictionary output
Definition 2(Definition in SDVD (DoD)).In SDVD, adefinitionis the output string returned
by the named dictionary when queried with a word. Two raw definitions are different if even one
character differs.
This is intentionally syntactic. It separatesdictionary content(strings) from whatever mental,
physical, or social phenomena those strings may trigger.
3.2 Meaning:meaningis what an utterance does
A dictionary entry is a string; meaning is not required to be a string. The VD proposal treats
meaning as a causal notion: the effect of an utterance on the world.
Definition 3(Meaning in SDVD (DoM)).In SDVD, themeaningof a word is the total change
in the relevant state of the world (physical, cognitive, computational, etc.) caused by the utterance
of that word in a given context.
Remark 2(Wavefunction as an instantiation).In quantum-mechanical settings, the “relevant
state” may be represented as a wavefunction; in other settings it may be a belief state, internal
robot registers, a social/behavioural state, or even the acoustic field. The definition is intended
to bemodel-agnostic: choose the state representation appropriate to the discussion.
3.3 Physical law: a triplet entry (structural definition)
The definition of physical law is kept purely structural: it describes a pattern of dictionary
introduction.
Definition 4(Physical law in SDVD).Aphysical lawis thefirst entryin atripletof
dictionary entries in which:
(i) the first word is introduced together with (and explicitly evokes) two previously undefined
terms, and
(ii) the three terms aremutually locked: none can be fully defined without reference to the
other two.

This definition intentionally does not attempt to encode truth, experiment, or utility. Those
topics may be discussed as external selection pressures on which triplets are retained by a

4 The Punnet Square: tracking definition/meaning interactions
Words like “definition” and “meaning” are themselves words whose behaviour must be tracked.
A compact bookkeeping device is thePundit Square, a 2×2 grid:
Definition of Definition (DoD) Definition of Meaning (DoM)
Meaning of Definition (MoD) Meaning of Meaning (MoM)
4.1 Pedagogical order
A practical point: many readers interpret “definition” as “mathematical characterisation” by
default. To avoid this confusion, a good learning order is:
DoD→DoM→MoD→MoM.
First nail down the syntactic dictionary notion of definition; then ground meaning as causal
effect; only then explain how the word “definition” behaves in human/mathematical practice;
finally explain the meta-behaviour of the word “meaning”.
5 DoM with examples: meaning as causal impact
Under DoM, an utterance can have many types of effects:
•Physical:air vibrations, recordings, signals in wires, actuation on sensors.
•Cognitive:recalling a stored definition, shifting attention, changing expectations.
•Behavioural/social:changing decisions, eliciting actions, coordinating groups.
•Computational:updating internal registers, running a procedure, logging an event.
Thus meaning is not reduced to a private mental “feeling” nor to a static semantic object; it is
the world-state update induced by an utterance.
A key interaction with the dictionary is this: uttering a word can increase the probability
that listeners explicitly realise (or “load into working memory”) particular definition strings
stored in their own internal dictionaries.
6 MoD: mathematical equality vs raw definitional identity
A recurring confusion is that the word “definition” often means something like “characterisation
up to mathematical equality”. VD makes it possible to separate these notions cleanly.
6.1 Raw definitional identity
By construction, raw definitional identity is character-level identity of strings. If δ1 and δ2 differ
by one character, they are distinct raw definitions.

6.2 Mathematical equality is a different relation
In ordinary mathematics one writes
4 = 2 + 2 = 1 + 3.
This is not a claim that the strings “4”, “2+2”, and “1+3” are identical definitions; they are
not. Instead the equality sign “=” is carrying amathematical equalityrelation, e.g. equality of
value in (N,+).
Remark 3(VD is compatible with mathematics).VD does not reject mathematical language;
it clarifies that mathematical “=” is not raw string identity. Mathematics is an arena where
both definitions and the operational meaning of expressions are unusually tightly policed.
7 MoM: why language works, why debates break, and how VD
helps
Themeaning of meaningis the recognition that (i) definitions are private and variable, while (ii)
communication succeeds because thecausal effectsof utterances (meanings) overlap sufficiently
across agents.
7.1 Different definitions, aligned meanings
Two people almost never store identical definition strings for a word; yet they can coordinate
because utterances push them into similar enough predictive/behavioural states. Communication
is therefore primarily supported by overlap of meanings, not identity of raw definitions.
7.2 Why natural language is easy and mathematics is hard
Natural language tolerates large definitional variation because approximate meaning-overlap
often suffices in context. Mathematics is difficult precisely because it demands tighter policing:
both definitions and the operational meaning of expressions (procedures performed by people
and machines) must align sharply to support long inference chains.
7.3 Why apparent agreement collapses in debate
Casual agreement can be an illusion: agents may share enough meaning-overlap for everyday
coordination while harbouring divergent definitions that only become visible under pressure
(debate, edge cases, formal derivation). VD diagnoses this as a mismatch between definition
alignment and meaning alignment.
7.4 Yielding authority to SDVD as a repair protocol
If disputants yield authority to a shared named dictionary (e.g. SDVD), they gain a protocol:
(i) index claims to explicit entries (stop smuggling hidden authorities),
(ii) separate definitional disagreement (dictionary edits/splits) from empirical disagreement
(world facts),
(iii) split overloaded symbols/words into distinct entries when needed,
(iv) continue discussion under explicit rules despite language slipperiness.

8 Triplets and physical law structure
8.1 Abstract triplet pattern
Let D be a named dictionary. A triplet involves three words ( w0, w1, w2) and three entries D(wi)
such that:
(i)w 0 is introduced by an entry that explicitly invokes two previously undefined terms w1, w2,
(ii)D(w 1) referencesw 0 andw 2,
(iii)D(w 2) referencesw 0 andw 1,
so that the dependency graph forms a 3-cycle. This is the minimal closure structure preventing
infinite definitional regress.
Previously defined (relative to an entry index).Fix an entry-log {E0, E1, . . .}, where
En is the nth submitted dictionary entry. At the moment we are about to add En, we say a
word (raw string) w ispreviously definediff there exists some k < n such that Ek is an entry
whosedefined word(the word on the left / the headline of the entry) is exactly w. Equivalently:
w already has its own dictionary entry prior to stage n. (We distinguish this from the weaker
notionpreviously mentioned, meaning w occurs anywhere inside earlier entry-texts; for the
governance rules below, “previously defined” is the operative notion.)
Law triplet.Alaw tripletis a package of three entries which jointly lock three words into
mutual definability. Concretely, a law triplet introduces two new words (thewinters) relative to
a previously defined bridge word, and provides three entries such that each of the three words is
defined in terms of the other two. In best practice these three entries are logged consecutively
(so they are recognisable as one unit), but the underlying structure is the existence of the three
mutual entries, not the typography of the lecture.
Recognisability and completion of law triplets.A single entry mayintendto be part of
a physical law without yet beingrecognisableas such from the dictionary alone. This is because
a law, in the strict SDVD sense, is not a lone statement but atripletof mutually defining entries:
until the other two entries are present, the reader (or an automated checker) cannot in general
certify that the requisite three-cycle exists, nor determine unambiguously which words are being
locked together by the law. Accordingly, we distinguish between acandidate law-entry(the
first entry of a prospective triplet) and acompleted law-triplet(all three entries logged). As a
best practice, whenever one records a candidate law-entry one should promptly follow it by the
remaining two entries, ideally as a consecutive block in the entry-log. This does not change
the logical content of the theory, but it greatly improves auditability, teaching clarity, and the
mechanical verification of “previously defined” constraints.
Explicit numbered example.Assume that by some earlier point we have already defined the
bridge word net force; i.e. there exists an index k such that Ek has defined word net force.
We now log a law triplet at the next three indices:
Ek+1 (November entry)net force:= mass· acceleration,
Ek+2 (January entry)mass:=net force/acceleration,
Ek+3 (February entry)acceleration:=net force/mass.
Here the underscore-marked strings mass and acceleration in Ek+1 are placeholders indicating
that these are intended asnewwords at the time the bridge entry is first stated; they are then

The November–January–February convention (the winters).For any law triplet, we
adopt the following role-names for the three defined words:
• The word defined by thefirstentry of the triplet is called theNovember word(role-index
0). It must bepreviously definedin the dictionary, and functions as the bridge linking this
law to earlier material.
• The words defined by thesecondandthirdentries are called theJanuary word(role-index
1) and theFebruary word(role-index 2). Together they are calledthe winters, i.e. the
paired new words introduced (or at least firstlocked) by the law triplet.
This convention allows us to refer quickly to governance constraints such as: “the November
word must already be defined; the winters must be new (or else require refactor/renaming).” It
also supports retroactive reconstruction: a lecturer may present any one of the three equations
first, but the triplet can be oriented by identifying which participating word is already defined
in the existing dictionary (the November word), thereby determining which two words are the
winters.
Naming the entries themselves.Finally, we name the three entries of a law triplet by the
month of the word they define: the entry which defines the November word is theNovember
entry; the entry which defines the January word is theJanuary entry; and the entry which
defines the February word is theFebruary entry.
8.2 Triplets can chain
Large mathematical statements can be decomposed into networks of triplets (by introducing
intermediate names for subexpressions). In practice one often keeps equations compact to avoid
naming explosion, while recognising that a triplet decomposition exists in principle.
9 Laws, operations, and “meaningful relationships”
A physical or mathematical law is not merely a list of definitions; it also specifies operations and
relations that tell a capable agent what to do with numbers or procedures.
Even in arithmetic, defining an addition operator does not by itself make the statement
“2 + 2 = 4” hold as a raw definitional identity. Rather, the equation asserts that when a capable
system engages the relevant computation under the accepted rules, the values coincide. This
illustrates a general point: the non-term symbols in laws (“+”, “=”, “proportional to”, etc.)
carry binding operational meaning.
(1) Symbol overloading and dictionary collisions.Physics notation often reuses the same
glyph for distinct quantities (e.g. writing F in both F = ma and F = kx), relying on human
intuition and context to disambiguate. In VD terms this is adictionary collision: multiple
concepts are implicitly mapped onto the same symbol-entry, and meaning is patched in “by vibe”
rather than by explicit definition. A VD-aligned discipline therefore prefers naming hygiene (e.g.
Fnet, FT , Fg) and explicit splits of overloaded symbols, because otherwise definitional drift is
easy, learning becomes harder, and even capable reasoning systems can silently conflate distinct
entries.

(2) “Definition” in ordinary practice vs. SDVD “definition”.Many readers hear
the worddefinitionand import a mathematical notion (“characterisation up to mathemati-
cal equality”) or an essentialist notion (“the true nature of the thing”). SDVD deliberately
starts with a narrower syntactic primitive:definitionmeans the literal output string of a
named dictionary, with identity determined character-by-character. The pedagogical order
DoD→DoM→MoD→MoM is designed to prevent confusion: one first fixes the string-level object,
then grounds meaning as causal impact, and only then explains how mathematical and everyday
uses of “definition” relate to (and often overload) the SDVD usage.
(3) Equality is overloaded: raw identity vs. mathematical equality.The glyph “=”
carries multiple distinct relations across domains. Under raw definition, equality isstring identity:
two definitions are equal only if their character strings are identical. In mathematics, however,
“=” typically denotes avalue-equalityrelation inside a structure (e.g. equality of denotation in
(N, +)), allowing different strings such as “2 + 2” and “1 + 3” to be equal even though they
are not identical as strings. VD is compatible with mathematics precisely because it forces
this disambiguation: whenever “=” is used, one must make explicit which equality relation is
intended rather than silently sliding between them.
10 Outlook (not yet finalised)
The present notes motivate several necessary extensions:
•Governance rules:how laws are added, revised, restricted in scope, or removed.
• Question-answering:how “why?” chains traverse triplets and where closure is obtained.
• Causality:how causal language emerges from update rules, interventions, and directed
traversals.
• Stopping regress:formalising why repeated “why?” loops around triplets unless anchored
by external procedures.
