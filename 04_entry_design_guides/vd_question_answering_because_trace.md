# VD Question Answering - The Because-Trace Layer

Status: working note, 2026-05-01.

This note records the question-answering layer of the VD. It complements the
normal trace/evaluator picture.

## Core Idea

The VD can answer textbook mechanics questions by running the trace on the
normalised question.

For explain/describe questions, use one small presentation tweak:

```text
Before each next demanded step, write:
And that's because:
```

The result is a chain of sentences that exposes why the answer follows.

If the entries are well written, this because-trace should contain the
important physics content needed to answer mechanics explanation questions.
The remaining work is presentation:

- remove steps too obvious to receive credit;
- combine repeated steps;
- smooth grammar;
- adapt wording to the exam/question style.

## Why This Works

The trace is already an answer-production route. It exposes the interpreter to
the headwords, residual handles, witness demands, inputs, computations, and
halts that produce the answer.

Adding "And that's because:" turns that route into an explanation chain:

```text
answer claim
And that's because: demanded entry/relation/input
And that's because: deeper demanded entry/relation/input
And that's because: ...
```

The explanation is not generated by freeform textbook recall. It is generated
by the same controlled exposure sequence that produced the answer.

## Normalisation Still Comes First

A natural-language question must first be normalised to a headword.

Examples:

```text
What is the acceleration of p?
  -> inertial-acceleration(p)

Why does a free massive particle have zero acceleration in an inertial frame?
  -> inertial-acceleration(p) under empty interacting-forces-set

Describe the forces on p.
  -> interacting-forces-set(p)
```

Different normalisations produce different explanation chains.

## Sketch

For a simple Newton II question:

```text
The inertial-acceleration of p is net-force(p) divided by inertial-mass(p).
And that's because: inertial-acceleration is defined by the Newton II triplet
as net-force divided by inertial-mass for a point-particle in an inertial-frame.

The net-force on p is the vector sum of the impressed-force elements in its
interacting-forces-set.
And that's because: the force-sum triplet defines net-force through the
interacting-forces-set and impressed-force.

The spring force is included in the interacting-forces-set.
And that's because: p's mechanical-composition contains a spring acting-object
whose activation-condition is satisfied and whose canonical-force produces an
impressed-force on p.
```

The raw because-trace may be clunky. That is acceptable. The first goal is
structural correctness. Polished prose is a later rendering step.

## Entry-Writing Consequence

Entries should be written so their trace expansions can become explanation
sentences.

This adds a practical test:

```text
If this entry appears after "And that's because:", does it give a useful,
credit-bearing reason?
```

Not every exposed step should survive into a final answer. Some steps are too
obvious or too infrastructural. But the raw trace must contain enough
credit-bearing material that pruning and grammar smoothing can produce a good
explanation.

## Relation To Complete Theory Representation

If this works, the VD is not merely a solver for Newtonian mechanics. It is a
complete representation of the important information in Newtonian mechanics
for problem-answering purposes.

It can produce:

- numerical or symbolic answers;
- derivation traces;
- explanation chains;
- describe-style force/object accounts;
- informative halts at theory boundaries;
- visible locations for assumptions and inputs.

In this sense, the VD's question-answering capacity is one of the strongest
tests of whether the entries really encode the theory.
