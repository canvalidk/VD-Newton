## Supersession and scope

This document is intended to become the current explanation of why a VD law has
the local form of one previously admitted November word and two co-born winter
words.

It **supersedes the attempted alignment** in sections 5 and 6 of
`triplet_as_admission_event_d1.md`. That note correctly treated the triplet as an
admission event, but it derived a meta-level history triplet,
`(residual-before, admitted-item, residual-after)`, and acknowledged that its
mapping onto `(anchor, winter, winter)` was not tight. The before/item/after
description remains useful as a record of catalogue change. It is not the three
headwords of the law itself.

This document **preserves and sharpens** the central insight of that note: the
unit of theoretical admission is not an isolated new word. It gives the three
law-headwords their own semantic derivation.

It does not supersede `alpha_and_the_one_draft_d1.md`. The alpha argument says
when evidence is strong enough for a candidate to leave the residual. This note
says what must be made explicit when the theory admits the distinction. Alpha
governs the warrant; the triplet records the content of the admission.

It also does not supersede `vd_six_entry_structure.md`. It supplies a proposed
justification for the inward triplet and for the local one-November/two-winter
pattern. The chimney and walls remain necessary to connect those inward roles
to the rest of the dictionary.

The stronger claim that every physics theory can be completely reconstructed in
this form remains a research hypothesis. The argument below identifies why the
rule is natural and why it is needed if a theory is to admit non-idle terms
honestly. It does not establish the hypothesis merely by declaring it.

---

## Thesis

A theory cannot enlarge its ontology merely by inventing a word. A new word
must make a distinction that the theory could not make before, and the theory
must state what licenses that distinction.

The minimum honest record therefore contains three roles:

1. a previously available candidate, phenomenon, problem, or state;
2. a newly admitted result, kind, quantity, or state; and
3. a newly articulated differentiator, solution, coefficient, operation, or
   relational condition by which the first is related to the second.

Call these roles (A), (B), and (C). Their content is the ternary relation

$$
T(A,B,C),
$$

which can often be oriented as

$$
B = R(A,C).
$$

In the VD, (A) is the November word. It already has a peripheral definition
and provides the point of contact with the existing catalogue. (B) and (C)
are winter words. They are admitted together because each supplies the
theoretical work the other would otherwise lack.

This is the triplet rule. The triangle in the dependency graph is not its
ultimate justification. The triangle is what appears when the minimum complete
admission relation is written without hiding a participant.

---

## 1. The route by which the justification emerged

The argument was not reached by beginning with triangles and searching for a
reason to prefer them. It emerged in stages.

### 1.1 The alpha route: admission has a before and an after

`alpha_and_the_one_draft_d1.md` begins with a catalogue of distinctions already
treated as real and one residual item for everything not yet distinguished. A
candidate crosses from the residual into the catalogue only when the available
evidence clears an admission policy or threshold.

That picture made an important fact visible: admission is an event. There is a
catalogue before it, something admitted, and a catalogue after it. The first
triplet note therefore proposed:

```text
residual-before -- admitted item -- residual-after
```

This correctly rejects the picture of a catalogue growing by contextless atoms.
But it describes the history of the catalogue at the meta-level. It does not yet
explain why a law inside the catalogue contains one old headword and two new
headwords. In particular, the third member of that historical triplet is the
updated residual, whereas the third member of a VD law is another theoretical
term.

The first route discovered the right phenomenon -- admission -- without yet
identifying the internal anatomy of what is admitted.

### 1.2 The E10 route: hidden commitments create circularity

The Newton-II mass work then exposed a practical consequence of failing to
separate a theory's constitutive relation from the evidence used to apply it.

If a measured net force is quietly required to be a force already reconciled
with acceleration, then measuring net force from component forces requires an
acceleration observation. If measured acceleration is likewise required to be
already Newton-II-compatible, then measuring acceleration from a trajectory
requires a force observation. The supposedly independent measurement channels
become mutually dependent, and Newton II is built into the inputs used to apply
or assess Newton II.

The repair was to distinguish:

```text
independent force-side measurement result
independent trajectory-side measurement result
joint Newton-II-constrained latent estimates
```

This showed why theoretical commitment and empirical determination cannot be
allowed to collapse into one statement. A theory may relate force,
acceleration, and mass. A measurement procedure must still say independently
what was observed, how quantities were estimated, and how well the independent
results conform to the relation.

That lesson is more general than E10. When a theory hides the procedure by which
a candidate is licensed as an instance of a theoretical term, it can make its
own law appear to be an observation. The missing admission structure is then a
source of circularity.

### 1.3 The iron route: the internal anatomy becomes visible

The decisive example was:

$$
\text{iron}
=
R(\text{suspected iron},\text{melting-point-of-iron}).
$$

Expanded schematically:

> If a suspected-iron material is determined to have a
> melting-point-of-iron, admit it as iron.

Before this commitment, *iron* is not yet a member of the theory's ontology.
This is not the metaphysical claim that the material did not exist. It is the
logical claim that the theory had not yet earned the distinction represented by
the word `iron`. The theory had only a prior candidate category such as
`suspected-iron`.

The new kind `iron` cannot be admitted alone. Alone it is merely a sound or a
label. The theory must also articulate what difference makes the new kind do
work. Here that second winter term is `melting-point-of-iron`. Conversely, the
kind-indexed property `melting-point-of-iron` has no theoretical role until
there is a kind `iron` for it to characterize and help admit. The kind and the
property are therefore co-born relative to the already available candidate
term.

This gives the exact local VD pattern:

```text
November / already admitted:  suspected-iron
Winter / newly admitted:      iron
Winter / newly admitted:      melting-point-of-iron
```

The alpha route supplied the candidate and the fact of admission. The E10 route
showed why constitutive relation and empirical determination must remain
distinct. The iron route finally showed why the admission itself has one prior
term and two co-admitted terms.

## 2. Why a new word by itself cannot do theoretical work

Suppose a theory with an existing vocabulary introduces a single new term
(B).

There are only three broad possibilities.

### 2.1 The new term is completely reducible to old vocabulary

If (B) has a complete definition using only already admitted terms, then it is
an abbreviation or derived convenience. It may be extremely useful, but it has
not added an irreducible theoretical distinction. In principle it can be
expanded away everywhere without changing the theory's commitments.

Such a definition can be a legitimate peripheral entry. It is not by itself a
new law.

### 2.2 The new term has no licensed relation to old vocabulary

If (B) is not reducible to old terms and no relation explains its admission,
then the theory has simply commanded the reader to accept a primitive. The word
does not solve the problem that motivated it; it renames the absence of a
solution.

This is word magic:

```text
We could not distinguish these cases.
We invented B.
Now we say the B-cases are distinguishable.
```

Nothing has changed except the vocabulary.

### 2.3 The new term is introduced by direct residue

A term may be grounded by an observation, demonstration, calibration, or other
human-executable residue procedure. That can license an outward-facing handle.
But an ostensive or operational handle does not by itself state a new law. It
says how a user can locate or measure something; it does not yet state the
irreducible relation by which the theory organizes it.

The triplet rule concerns that irreducible theoretical relation. It does not
claim that every new dictionary label, measurement result, initial condition,
or abbreviation must create a law-triplet.

The important case is therefore a term that is meant to be more than shorthand
but less than an unsupported primitive. Such a term must enter through a new
relation. For the relation to be anchored to what the theory could already say,
it needs an old term (A). For the new term (B) not to enter arbitrarily, it
needs a co-admitted term (C) that states the new distinction, operation, or
constraint. The result is (T(A,B,C)).

## 3. Why all three participants are needed

The necessity of the triplet can be tested by removing one role at a time.

### 3.1 Candidate plus admitted kind: (A) and (B), without (C)

```text
suspected-iron -> iron
```

This records a relabelling but no reason for it. Nothing states why one
suspected material crosses the boundary while another remains merely
suspected. The new kind is arbitrary.

### 3.2 Candidate plus differentiator: (A) and (C), without (B)

```text
suspected-iron + melting-point-of-iron
```

This may record a test or regularity, but the theory has not stated what new
distinction follows from it. There is a procedure with no admitted result. The
ontology has not changed.

### 3.3 Admitted kind plus differentiator: (B) and (C), without (A)

```text
iron has a melting-point-of-iron
```

This hides the pre-admission domain. It tells us something about things already
called iron, but not how the use of `iron` was earned. If the property is then
measured only on things already identified as iron, the apparent criterion can
be circular: the kind licenses the property assignment while the property is
said to license the kind.

### 3.4 Candidate, admitted kind, and differentiator: (A), (B), and (C)

```text
if an A is licensed as possessing C, admit it as B
```

Now the complete transition is visible:

- where the candidate stood before commitment;
- what new distinction the theory admits;
- and what makes that distinction non-arbitrary.

Each term does work that neither of the others can perform. Three is not a
decorative preference. It is the smallest complete record of this kind of
admission.

## 4. Why the two winter words are simultaneous

The winter words need not be invented at the same historical instant. One of
their spellings may have appeared in ordinary language centuries earlier. Their
simultaneity is logical rather than chronological.

Relative to the theory immediately before the admission:

- (B) does not yet have its new theoretical status without (C);
- (C) does not yet have its kind-specific theoretical role without (B).

The relation (T(A,B,C)) gives both roles at once.

In the iron example, generic heating, phase transition, temperature, and even a
generic procedure for determining melting points might already be available.
What is co-admitted with `iron` is the role
`melting-point-of-iron`: the property as indexed to and doing classificatory
work for this newly admitted kind. Reusing old experimental vocabulary does not
make that new theoretical role old.

This is also why an equation written with one term on the left should not be
mistaken for a temporal creation order. The underlying object is the ternary
relation (T(A,B,C)). Writing (B=R(A,C)) is one orientation of it. A different
question may orient the same relation around another corner.

The VD records all three inward-facing definitions because no single linear
sentence preserves the whole mutual relation. The three definitions are the
three readable orientations of one admission, not three separately discovered
laws.

## 5. The constitutive commitment is not the measured value

The iron example also reveals a distinction ordinary scientific prose often
compresses.

The sentence

> Iron has a melting point of 1500 degrees.

looks like one proposition, but it fuses at least two different commitments.

### 5.1 Stable theoretical or ontological commitment

The theory admits `iron` together with a property-role
`melting-point-of-iron`, related to the prior candidate category. Schematically:

$$
T(\text{suspected iron},
  \text{iron},
  \text{melting-point-of-iron}).
$$

This is the triplet-level commitment. It says that the property exists as a
property of the admitted kind and participates in the distinction by which the
kind is recognized.

### 5.2 Revisable empirical determination

The best available experiment presently assigns a value to that property under
specified conditions, using a stated procedure, uncertainty, and provenance.
Schematically:

$$
\operatorname{BestEstimate}
(\text{melting-point-of-iron};
 \text{conditions},\text{method},\text{date})
=1500\ ^\circ\mathrm{C}\;\text{with uncertainty}.
$$

The number here is the deliberately schematic value from the motivating
example, not a claim of chemical precision.

The empirical determination may improve without re-admitting iron or rewriting
the triplet. If tomorrow's best estimate changes, the evidence ledger changes.
The ontology need not.

Putting the number inside the constitutive definition would create several
confusions:

- a refined measurement would look like a change in what iron is;
- uncertainty and experimental conditions would be hidden;
- a sample near the boundary could be declared non-iron merely because the
  current estimate was imperfect;
- the theory would appear to predict a value that was actually supplied by its
  measurement history.

The honest form of the old material-theory commitment is therefore not one
timeless statement, `iron has melting point 1500`. It is a stable property-role
commitment plus a revisable determination of the property's value.

## 6. A differentiator may be a dossier, not one property

Nothing in the triplet rule requires real scientific kinds to be identified by
one perfectly unique scalar property. Melting point is useful because it makes
the logical roles easy to see, not because it is asserted to be a sufficient
modern identification procedure for iron.

In a realistic case, (C) may be a structured dossier:

```text
licensed differentiator C =
  measurement procedures
  + uncertainty models
  + characteristic-property results
  + exclusion tests
  + provenance
  + an admission policy
```

The dossier can remain one theoretical participant because it performs one
role in the admission: it is the structured basis on which an (A)-candidate
is or is not admitted as (B). Complexity inside a participant does not alter
the arity of the relation among the roles.

This matters for the alpha connection. Alpha, a dossier budget, or another
policy does not create the kind by itself. It decides whether the evidence for
the (C)-role is adequate in a particular case. The triplet supplies the kind
of transition that the evidence is being asked to license.

## 7. How this explains the six-entry law house

The admission argument makes the order of the six-entry unit intelligible.

### Chimney: the prior candidate must already be sayable

The November word (A) needs a peripheral definition before the law. Without
it, there is no previously intelligible problem, phenomenon, or candidate to
which the admission can attach. All three terms would float free of the
existing catalogue.

### Triplet: the old term is put into a new relation with two co-born terms

The inward entries record (T(A,B,C)) from each corner. The November word is not
invented again, but it acquires a new relational definition. The two winter
words receive their first definitions through the same closure.

### Walls: the new terms become independently usable

After admission, (B) and (C) need outward-facing definitions that connect
them to earlier procedures, observations, types, or concepts. These walls do
not retroactively make the winter words old. They make the newly admitted terms
usable outside the law that co-introduced them.

The resulting order is natural:

```text
make the prior problem/candidate available
-> admit the new distinction and its solution/differentiator together
-> ground the two new terms for later use
```

The house is therefore not merely a graph pattern. It is the temporal record of
an honest conceptual extension.

## 8. What the Haskell and graph arguments do -- and do not -- establish

The existing VD material contains two neighbouring arguments:

1. a two-cycle is said to be trivial while a three-cycle is the smallest
   non-trivial cycle; and
2. mutually recursive triplet definitions produce a loop when directly encoded
   in a lazy functional program.

Both observations help describe the completed triplet. Neither is by itself a
sufficient justification of the rule.

A two-variable algebraic relation can be non-trivial, and a two-node recursive
program can loop. Looping therefore does not uniquely select three nodes.
Likewise, encoding three algebraically equivalent orientations of one equation
shows mutual dependence, but it does not by itself explain why a theory must
introduce exactly two winter words beside one anchor.

The admission argument supplies what those structural observations lack. A
binary relation may connect two things, but it cannot simultaneously display:

- the pre-existing domain against which novelty is assessed;
- the newly admitted theoretical distinction; and
- the condition, operation, or co-concept that makes the distinction do work.

Once those three semantic roles are required, the 3-cycle and the functional
loop are expected representations of their mutual dependence. They become
corroborating descriptions rather than the foundational proof.

There is a second caution. Mutual theoretical definition does not imply that
all empirical measurements of the related quantities must be jointly produced.
In Newton II, force-side and trajectory-side measurement results can and must
remain independently meaningful even when they do not exactly satisfy the
triplet. Joint estimation is a later, theory-constrained operation. Confusing
semantic dependence inside a law with dependence among observation channels is
exactly the error found in E10.

## 9. What the rule prevents

The triplet rule is an audit discipline. It makes several common evasions
visible.

### Word magic

A new label appears without a stated difference that earns it.

### Hidden primitives

A theory presents a term as derived while one of the terms needed to license it
is left implicit and therefore functions as an unacknowledged primitive.

### Circular identification

The theory says that members of (B) possess (C), then uses (C) as evidence
for (B), without preserving the prior (A)-candidate state or an independent
evidential procedure.

### Theory-measurement collapse

A constitutive claim that a kind has a measurable property is fused with the
current numerical estimate of that property.

### Self-confirming measurement

Measured inputs are required by definition to satisfy the very law they are
supposed to assess, so disagreement is erased before it can be recorded.

### Idle ontology

A noun is allowed into the catalogue even though removing it would change no
inference, classification, prediction, or licensed operation.

In each case, asking for (A), (B), (C), and (T(A,B,C)) locates the hidden
commitment. Either the triplet can be completed, or the theory must admit that
the alleged new term is only shorthand, residue, or an unsupported posit.

## 10. The justified claim and the open claim

The argument justifies the following local principle:

> Whenever a theory makes a genuinely new, non-reducible distinction from a
> previously articulable domain, an honest record must preserve the prior
> candidate, the newly admitted term, and the co-admitted theoretical basis on
> which the distinction does work.

That is the triplet rule in semantic form.

It supports, but does not yet prove, the stronger VD hypothesis:

> Every irreducible law-bearing enlargement of a physical theory can be
> normalized as a triplet containing exactly one previously admitted headword
> and two co-admitted headwords.

The stronger hypothesis is scientifically interesting because it is
falsifiable as a reconstruction programme. For any alleged counterexample, ask:

1. What could the theory say before introducing the term?
2. Is the new term merely eliminable shorthand?
3. If it is not shorthand, what licenses the distinction it draws?
4. Does that licensing concept already exist in the required role, or is it
   logically co-admitted with the new term?
5. Can the complete commitment be written as (T(A,B,C))?
6. Have numerical estimates, observation records, or policy thresholds been
   mistaken for constitutive definitions?

If a genuinely irreducible theoretical admission survives those questions but
requires fewer than three semantic roles, the necessity claim is weakened. If
it requires more, the investigation should ask whether one role is a structured
dossier, whether several admissions have been compressed together, or whether
the VD's local arity is genuinely too small.

The argument also does not yet derive every global ledger rule. In particular,
it explains why one admission has one prior anchor and two co-born terms. It
does not by itself prove that the same headword can never anchor a second law at
a later point in the log. The global unique-November constraint still requires
its own argument about whether a later use is the same theoretical role or a
newly refined candidate state.

---

## Conclusion

The natural unit of theoretical invention is not a word. It is a licensed new
distinction.

A licensed distinction requires somewhere to begin, something newly sayable,
and a basis on which the transition between them does work. In the VD these are
the November word and two winter words. Remove the November word and the new
terms have no declared relation to the prior theory. Remove either winter word
and the remaining new word becomes either idle or arbitrary.

The triplet rule is therefore natural in a precise sense: it is the smallest
structure that prevents a theory from pretending that naming is explaining.

The original alpha argument showed that a candidate crosses a boundary. The
mass-estimation problem showed that the theory's relation must not be confused
with the evidence used to apply it. The iron example showed the complete event:

$$
\text{newly admitted kind}
=
R(\text{prior candidate},\text{co-admitted distinguishing role}).
$$

The catalogue changes only when that whole relation is licensed. What enters is
not an atom bearing a fresh name. What enters is a closed piece of theoretical
work.
